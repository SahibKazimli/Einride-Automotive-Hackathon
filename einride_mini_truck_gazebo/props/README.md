# Vendored arena props

Third-party 3D assets from [Gazebo Fuel](https://app.gazebosim.org/fuel/models),
vendored into this package so the arena world works **offline, forever**: a fresh
clone plus `colcon build` plus launch needs no `~/.gz/fuel` cache and no network.

Only `meshes/` and `materials/` are upstream. The `model.sdf` and `model.config` in
each installed prop are **ours**, generated from `../prop_template/` — see
[Why we do not ship upstream's SDF](#why-we-do-not-ship-upstreams-sdf).

Upstream authors, from each `upstream.model.config.txt`: **Cole Biesemeyer** and
**Nate Koenig**. Retrieved 2026-09-11.

All three are **CC0-1.0** — public domain dedication, *no attribution required*.
Credited anyway, and the full legal text is in [`LICENSE.CC0`](LICENSE.CC0). Because
CC0 imposes no obligations, the table above exists for **reproducibility**, not
compliance: it is what lets a future reader establish exactly which bytes are here.

Re-fetch with `../tools/fetch_props.sh` (needs network; run by hand, never at build
time). Verify the bytes never changed:

```bash
cd einride_mini_truck_gazebo/props && sha256sum -c MANIFEST.sha256
```

> **Version pinning is advisory.** `fetch_props.sh` passes a pinned version in the
> URL, but the `gz fuel download` shipped with ROS 2 Jazzy warns *"currently only the
> model's latest (tip) version is supported"* and ignores it. The versions above are
> what tip resolved to on the retrieval date and are checked against the cache
> directory name. **`MANIFEST.sha256` is what actually pins the bytes** — if upstream
> moves, the manifest check fails loudly instead of the arena changing silently.

## What we changed

- `meshes/`, `materials/` — **upstream, byte-for-byte.** Listed in `MANIFEST.sha256`.
  The `.dae` files reference their textures as `../materials/textures/…`, i.e.
  relatively, so vendoring the two directories side by side needs no path rewriting.
- `model.sdf`, `model.config` — **replaced by ours**, generated per prop from
  `../prop_template/` with the scale from `props.cmake`.
- `upstream.model.sdf.txt`, `upstream.model.config.txt` — upstream's originals, kept
  for review and future comparison. The `.txt` suffix is deliberate: it keeps the
  pristine copy in the diff while making it impossible for `gz sim` to load.
- `AABB.md`, `measured.json` — generated by `../tools/dae_measure.py` and **committed**,
  so the measurement is a recorded fact rather than something re-derived by hand.
- `mesh.txt`, `upstream_scale.txt` — the mesh path and upstream's own `<mesh><scale>`,
  so CMake does not have to glob or parse XML.

## Why we do not ship upstream's SDF

Each of these was found by reading the asset, not guessed at.

**`jersey_barrier`'s collision is not a mesh.** It is six hand-written boxes at
`4.06542 × … × 1.143` m. `<mesh><scale>` does nothing to them, so scaling the visual
would leave a 732 mm barrier with a **4 m invisible wall** across the arena.

**`construction_barrel` is not static.** Mass 500 kg, a hand-written inertia tensor
for the full 1.02 × 1.55 m barrel, and `<inertial><pose>0 0 0.4</pose>`. Scale scales
neither mass nor inertia nor that pose, so at 0.18 you get a 279 mm body weighing half
a tonne whose centre of mass sits 121 mm *outside its own geometry*.

**Sensors ray-cast against visuals, collisions do not.** `gz-sim`'s `gpu_lidar` and
`camera` are rendering sensors, while the planner collides with `<collision>`. A
visual/collision scale mismatch therefore makes the LiDAR see one arena and the
planner hit another, with nothing in the logs. Our template substitutes one
`@PROP_SCALE@` into both, so the mismatch is unrepresentable.

Our props are all `<static>true</static>` with the `<inertial>` block deleted outright
rather than left behind, so flipping the flag cannot silently resurrect an impossible
body. Static is also the cheap choice: `arena.sdf` runs `max_step_size 0.001`, so a
dynamic mesh collision would cost a narrowphase query every millisecond of simulated
time and buy nothing — a 2.4 kg rover cannot move any of these.

## Measured geometry

Measured with `../tools/dae_measure.py`, at the scale each prop is actually placed at.
Full detail per prop in `<dir>/AABB.md`.

| Prop | Upstream (mm) | Scale | Placed (mm) | At z=145 mm | Returns @1 m / @4 m |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `construction_barrel` | 1020 × 1020 × 1550 | 0.18 | 184 × 184 × 279 | 99 × 99 | 7.1 / 1.8 |
| `jersey_barrier` | 4066 × 813 × 1143 | 0.18 | 732 × 146 × 206 | 732 × 63 | 52.4 / 13.1 |
| `construction_cone` | 503 × 503 × 1089 | 2.50 | 126 × 126 × 272 | 59 × 59 | 4.2 / 1.1 |

All three span the whole mandated 80–180 mm scan band from the floor up, and all stay
below the 300 mm arena wall. Three things about this table are worth knowing:

- **All three are authored in inches** (`<unit meter="0.0254">`). Ignoring that gives
  an answer 39.37× too large that still looks plausible.
- **The cone's scale is 2.50, not 0.25.** Its upstream SDF already applies
  `<scale>10 10 10</scale>`, so the raw mesh is a tenth of nominal.
- **The AABB is not the number that matters** — the cross-section at the scan plane
  is. A cone scaled to 0.12 has an AABB that "covers" 80–180 mm while being only
  131 mm tall, so the scan plane at 145 mm misses it entirely and the LiDAR sees
  nothing. `dae_measure.py` reports the slab cross-section for exactly this reason.

The cone is a **deliberately weak LiDAR landmark**: under 2 returns beyond 4 m, below
the 3 needed to fit anything. It is carried for its orange/white diffuse map, i.e. as
a *camera* feature. The barrel and barrier do the LiDAR work.

## Rejected: NIST fiducial barrel

[NIST fiducial barrel](https://app.gazebosim.org/OpenRobotics/fuel/models/NIST%20fiducial%20barrel)
(CC0, 50 KB) was the most appealing candidate found — literally "NIST standard test
arena fiducial barrel". It does not work:

- Its mesh content sits at **z 300–1193 mm and y 292–590 mm relative to its own
  origin**, with no compensating `<pose>` in its SDF. In Gazebo it hangs in mid-air,
  off-centre, and contributes nothing to the 80–180 mm band.
- It is an **open shell** — 90 vertices, a single "Plane" geometry — so a mesh
  collision on it is meaningless.

To use it anyway it needs `<pose>0 -s*0.441 -s*0.300 0 0 0</pose>` and a scale in
roughly 0.151–0.267 to put any of it in the band.

## Rejected: referencing Fuel URLs directly

`<include><uri>https://fuel.gazebosim.org/1.0/OpenRobotics/models/Construction Cone</uri></include>`
is the idiomatic Gazebo approach and is what 46 of the 125 worlds shipped with gz-sim
do. It needs no vendoring and costs no repo weight — but it needs network on first
launch, makes that launch slow, and lets the upstream asset change under you. Recorded
here so nobody "simplifies" back to it without knowing what it trades away.
